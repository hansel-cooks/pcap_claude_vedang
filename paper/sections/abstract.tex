\begin{abstract}
Diabetic retinopathy screening depends on fast and reliable segmentation of retinal vessels, and the
multi-scale Frangi vesselness filter is a training-free classical method whose cost grows with image resolution
and with the number of scales. We implement a complete pipeline (green-channel extraction, CLAHE, multi-scale
Frangi filtering, thresholding and morphological clean-up) in C, parallelise every stage with OpenMP, and
compare it stage by stage with a pragma-free serial build of the same code on a 2-core, 4-thread cloud CPU.
Serial and OpenMP outputs are bit-identical on all 20 DRIVE test images for every thread count and design
variant. With parameters tuned on the DRIVE training images only, the pipeline reaches a mean Dice of 0.713 on
the DRIVE test set and 0.654 on STARE. The Gaussian-derivative passes take 72\% of the 108\,ms serial time.
Four threads speed the whole pipeline up by 2.08$\times$ (19.2 images per second), but placement matters: two
threads on the two hyperthreads of one core give 1.08$\times$, against 1.98$\times$ when they are spread over
both cores, and Hyper-Threading adds only 7\%. Owner-computes tile histograms are 1.68$\times$ faster than atomic
histograms in CLAHE, and running the four Frangi scales concurrently is 1.17$\times$ slower than parallelising
inside each scale because of load imbalance. The 4-thread speedup stays between 2.13 and 2.31 from 0.33 to 21.1
megapixels. We report per-stage Karp--Flatt serial fractions and throughput, and place the accuracy beside
published lightweight deep models.
\end{abstract}
